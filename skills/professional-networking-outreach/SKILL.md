# Professional Networking Outreach

## Boundary

Support human networking, not bulk messaging. Do not send messages, scrape member lists, claim a relationship, imply a referral, or use platform features without user approval. Respect each platform's rules, group norms, privacy expectations, and rate limits.

## Workflow

1. Identify the user's networking goal: referral path, market learning, event follow-up, hiring manager research, community presence, or warm reactivation.
2. Extract the target context: person, company, event, group, role, shared connection, shared interest, or prior interaction.
3. Choose the channel: LinkedIn connection note, LinkedIn message, Meetup event intro, post-event follow-up, alumni note, community DM, email, or calendar ask.
4. Draft a short message grounded in verified facts and a low-friction ask.
5. Track the next action: send, wait, follow up, prepare questions, attend event, or close the loop.

## Message Standards

- Lead with the real reason for reaching out.
- Use one concrete shared context point when available.
- Keep the ask easy: 10-15 minutes, one question, a pointer to the right person, or permission to follow up.
- Avoid over-familiar language, flattery, pressure, and generic career-change scripts.
- Do not ask for a referral before establishing fit or context unless the user already has a real relationship.

## Event Networking

For Meetup, conferences, panels, hackathons, and community events:

- Prepare a short self-introduction tied to the event theme.
- Draft two or three questions that create a real conversation.
- Identify who to follow up with and why.
- Convert notes into one specific follow-up within 24-48 hours.
- Record what was discussed so future messages do not feel generic.

## Output

Return:

- Networking objective and target list.
- Channel-specific draft messages.
- Suggested conversation questions.
- Follow-up schedule or pipeline updates.
- Claims to avoid because they are unsupported.
